When does Oak Park report crime?

All 168 hour-by-weekday cells. An incident reported as a range contributes a fraction to each hour it covers rather than a whole incident to the first hour, which is why the cells hold decimals. The 168 cells sum to exactly the number of timed incidents, and a test enforces that.

Weather moves reported crime more than anything else here

Mean incidents per day inside each cohort, against the all-days baseline of 5.99 incidents per day. Warm bars run above the baseline, cool bars below. These cohorts overlap: a day can be freezing and a weekend at once, so the rows share days and must never be added together. Each is only comparable to the baseline.

Darkness explains less than people expect

Across all incidents the mean dark fraction is 0.365 against a baseline of 0.438: the average incident falls in civil darkness about 7 points less than its own day's night length would predict. Below, the offenses that bend furthest either way. Positive means the offense happens in darkness more than the season alone explains. Read it with the caveat that a wide reported window spreads its weight uniformly, so offenses usually discovered later rather than witnessed live drift darker than they were.

An exact timestamp is not an exact time

The open-data stream carries a precise occur_datetime, which invites reading it as a real instant. Times pile up on round values far past anything a real distribution would produce. Exactly midnight carries 55 times the uniform per-minute rate and exactly noon 50 times. Those two are the signature of a date-only record or a form defaulting to 12:00 AM, not of 523 incidents happening at the same second. Across 47 clustered values, 5,657 incidents sit on a value carrying at least three times the uniform rate, and 4,232 of those sit exactly on the hour: 31% of every timestamped incident. Read any hour-of-day figure with that in mind.

Where the two ingest streams came from

Two independent sources feed this dataset upstream, and they overlap only partly. The dashboard-only share is the reason the second stream exists, and the reason a third of incidents have no narrative text for topics or keyword flags to read.
